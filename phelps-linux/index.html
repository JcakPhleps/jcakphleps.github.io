Phelps Linux is a small, quick Linux I've been building. You flash it to a USB stick, plug it into a Mac or PC, and it boots into its own desktop — without touching whatever's already on the computer. If you like it, you can install it for real.

What it actually is

It's a real Linux distribution, not a theme or a web page. Under the hood it's Alpine Linux — tiny and fast — and on top of that is a desktop I made: a file manager, a text editor, a terminal that's a real shell, a calculator, notes, a calendar, a clock, paint, an image viewer, a music player, and Firefox and LibreOffice when you want them.

It keeps itself up to date over the internet, so once you've got it, you don't re-download anything — it just gets newer on its own.

Put it on a USB stick

You'll need a USB stick (4 GB or bigger). This erases the stick, so use an empty one.

  1. Download the file above and unzip it — you'll get phelps-linux.img.
  2. Flash it to the stick. The easy way is Balena Etcher (free, works on Mac, Windows, Linux): open it, pick the .img, pick your USB stick, hit Flash.
  3. If you'd rather use the terminal on a Mac, find the disk with diskutil list, then: 
    sudo dd if=phelps-linux.img of=/dev/rdiskN bs=4m
    (swap N for your USB's number — double-check it, this overwrites the whole disk.)

Boot it

  1. Plug the stick into the computer and restart it.
  2. On a Mac: hold the Option (⌥) key as it turns on, then pick the EFI Boot drive.
    On a PC: tap the boot-menu key (usually F12, F10, or Esc) and choose the USB stick.
  3. It boots into Phelps Linux. Nothing on the computer's own drive gets changed — pull the stick out and everything's back to normal.

What you need

A 64-bit computer

An Intel Mac, or a PC that boots with UEFI. (Apple-Silicon Macs aren't supported.)

About 2 GB of RAM

It's light. Old laptops are kind of the point.

A USB stick

4 GB or bigger, and empty — flashing wipes it.

A few minutes

Download, flash, reboot. That's the whole thing.

Before you try it

This is a work in progress. I'm one person and I'm still building it, so things will be rough and some stuff will break. That's expected.

If something goes wrong, or you just have thoughts, tell me — open an issue on GitHub. It genuinely helps.
